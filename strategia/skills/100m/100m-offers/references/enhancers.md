## 1. Why enhancers exist

Marketing shifts the demand curve to the right; the enhancers are the levers that do it: bonuses, scarcity, urgency, guarantees, naming (plus commitment and consistency, status, peer pressure, goodwill, endorsements, competition). When demand rises, raise price and cut supply. People want what they cannot easily have, and fear of loss outweighs desire for gain of the same size.

Every enhancer must be true. A fake countdown or fake "3 spots left" poisons the guarantee and the proof too, because the prospect now assumes everything is theatre.

## 2. Scarcity (quantity)

Three types: limited supply of the offer, limited supply of a bonus, never available again.

Physical products: limited releases no more than monthly, and always sell out (an unsold "limited" run signals low value).

Services:
- "We take X clients total."
- "We take X clients per week or month, and Y spots remain."
- "X per class or cohort."
- Highest leverage: a small number of 1-on-1 slots at a very high price.
- Exit scarcity: once you leave you cannot come back (drives retention).

Scarcity works because the ability to charge depends on how many people can deliver the outcome and how valuable the outcome is. People who do not need the sale can hold price; people who need it negotiate.

## 3. Urgency (time)

1. **Cohort-based rolling urgency**: "Sign up now and you start Monday, otherwise the next intake is in four weeks." Turning away late buyers costs less than it earns; they were not buying anyway.
2. **Rolling seasonal urgency**: promotions with hard end dates, repeated with a new name each month (spring, summer, back-to-school). The deadline must be honoured.
3. **Pricing or bonus-based urgency**: a bonus that expires, or a price that rises on a stated date.
4. **Exploding opportunity**: the same, but bigger and rarer.

Deadlines drive decisions. Local businesses should rotate mechanisms more often to avoid fatigue.

## 4. Bonuses

A single offer is worth less than the same offer split into named components stacked as bonuses. "$37 for this knife, and you get 10 more free" outsells "11 knives for $37".

Bonuses beat discounts: a discount teaches the market that the price is negotiable; a bonus gives X + Y for the same price.

In a 1-on-1 sale: do not reveal bonuses before the first close attempt. If the prospect hesitates, add a bonus sized to the specific obstacle in their mind.

For every bonus:
- Name it with the benefit in the title.
- Say how it relates to their problem, what it is, how you built it, how it changes their life.
- Give proof if you have it (real only).
- Paint the picture of life after using it.
- Put a price on it and justify the price.
- Attach scarcity or urgency to the bonus itself ("only with this programme, never sold separately", "only for the first N buyers", "only if you decide today").

Each bonus should target one specific objection or obstacle. The total stated value of the bonuses should eclipse the value of the core offer.

Advanced: other people's products. Approach complementary businesses and get their product or service free or discounted for your clients in exchange for exposure, and negotiate an affiliate fee on top. The bonus costs you nothing and can even pay you.

Good bonus material: anything expensive to build once but free to distribute (checklists, spreadsheets, calculators, templates, recorded workshops, interviews, guides).

## 5. Guarantees

The biggest reason people do not buy is the risk it will not work for them. A guarantee is the direct answer to that. Structure: "If you don't achieve X in Y time, we will Z."

### Four categories (book)

| Type | Description | Use when |
|---|---|---|
| Unconditional | Refund, no questions asked | You need maximum trust and can absorb refunds |
| Conditional | Refund or remedy if the client did the required actions | You know the actions that produce results; build them into the conditions |
| Anti-guarantee | "All sales final", with a reason that signals confidence or exclusivity | The product's value is consumed on delivery, or you want to repel bargain hunters |
| Implied (performance) | You are paid only when the result happens | Strongest of all; use when you control the outcome |

Stack guarantees the way you stack bonuses. Refund abuse is rarely the real risk: for a guarantee to lose money, refunds would have to equal the new customers it brought in. Conditions exist to filter people who buy only for the guarantee.

Name the guarantee. A strong named guarantee is itself proof: "If I did not deliver, this guarantee would have put me out of business."

### Twelve guarantee types (journal)

1. **Results**: refund, half back, return the ad spend, or pay for a competitor's programme.
2. **Service**: keep working with them free until they get the result (optionally with conditions).
3. **Double your service**: another full period free (twice what they paid for).
4. **Credit**: what they paid becomes credit toward any other service.
5. **Work with you personally**: the founder works 1-on-1 free until the result arrives.
6. **Pay plus ad spend**: refund the fee plus whatever you spent advertising for them.
7. **Hotel and airfare** (events): reimburse travel if they did not get value.
8. **Pay you for your time** (calls): pay their hourly rate if the call was not valuable.
9. **Early exit**: leave the contract free of charge.
10. **First outcome**: if no first result (for example a new client) within a short window such as 14 days, you cover the cost (for example ad spend) until they get it.
11. **Delayed second payment**: no further billing until after their first sale or first result.
12. **Performance**: pay only per unit of result delivered; no result, no payment.

### How to create an attractive guarantee (journal)

1. Write down every pain and obstacle prospects raise in the sale, and reverse each into a promise. Think about their time, their emotions, and their outside costs (ad spend, travel, opportunity cost).
2. The more specific it is, the more it stands out. "Money back if unhappy" is invisible; "if you do not add 20 new members in 30 days we keep working for free until you do, and we refund your ad spend" is remembered.

The only job of the guarantee is to remove the specific obstacle your prospects have. Pick the type by the obstacle.

## 6. Naming

The name must make people curious on hearing it. Change the name when it fatigues; never change the offer itself.

### MAGIC formula (book and journal)

| Letter | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| M | Make a magnetic reason (why now, why this) | Back to school, Free, 87% off, Guaranteed, Flash sale, Giveaway, Seasonal, Holiday, Local area, Avatar call-out |
| A | Announce the avatar | Dentists, Busy mums, Agency owners, South Austin |
| G | Give them a goal | Packed gym, Skinny jeans, First client, Pain-free, Double profit, Passive, Recurring |
| I | Indicate a time interval | 21 day, 6 week, 28 day, 90 minute, 3 attempts |
| C | Complete with a container word | Challenge, Transformation, Intensive, Project, Accelerator, Blueprint, Bootcamp, Incubator, Launch, Shortcut, Blast, Miracle, Shred, Crusher, Roadmap, Detox, Slimdown, System |

Use two or three parts, not all five. Shorter is better. Specific is better. Rhyme and alliteration help.

### Headline formula (journal's earlier draft, useful as a word bank)

Enhancer + Avatar + Duration + Dream Outcome + Power Word.

- Duration bank: number of days, weeks, months, hours, attempts.
- Outcome bank (write these in the avatar's words): bikini, little black dress, skinny jeans, jealous friend, packed gym, autopilot, passive, profit, recurring.
- Power word bank: challenge, transformation, intensive, project, accelerator, blueprint, bootcamp, incubator, launch, shortcut, blast, miracle, shred, crusher, roadmap, detox, slimdown.
- Enhancer bank: free, X% off, guarantee, flash sale, giveaway, seasonal, holiday, local area, avatar call-out.

Journal examples of the formula assembled: "Free 21 Day Detox", "28 Day Bikini Blueprint", "Summer 6 Week Toned Stomach Miracle", "South Austin Skinny Jeans Project", "6 Week Profit Project", "21 Day New Client Shortcut".

An alternative journal template for a hook: "How I got [good thing] without [bad thing] using something different."

## 7. Gift wrapping: same bundle, different wrapper

Journal sketch: the same value bundle presented as "Fall 6 Week Challenge", then "Spring 28 Day Blueprint", then a third wrapper. Nothing inside changed. Rotate the wrapper (name, season, enhancer, deadline) to keep demand fresh without rebuilding delivery.
