# Critique rubric for an existing offer

Score every line 0, 1, or 2. Quote the user's own copy as evidence wherever possible. Total is out of 30. Present the scorecard as a table, then name the single biggest leak, then rebuild.

| # | Criterion | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Avatar is specific | "Anyone", "businesses", "people who want X" | Industry or role named | Industry, role, size, and situation named; a reader can tell instantly whether it is for them |
| 2 | Market passes the four tests (pain, purchasing power, easy to target, growing) | Fails two or more | Fails one | Passes all four, and pain is stated in the copy |
| 3 | Dream outcome stated in the prospect's words | Features only | Outcome named but generic | Vivid, specific outcome plus the status it brings |
| 4 | Perceived likelihood: proof and specificity | No proof, vague claims | Some proof, not tied to "people like you" | Real, specific proof relevant to the avatar (never invented) |
| 5 | Time delay addressed | No mention of speed or first win | Speed claimed without mechanism | A defined fast first win and a stated timeline |
| 6 | Effort and sacrifice reduced | Client does most of the work | Some done-for-you elements | Clear removal of steps; DFY or DWY where it matters |
| 7 | Problems solved comprehensively | Solves one problem | Solves the main problems | Anticipates before, during, and after problems including the awkward ones (travel, eating out, no time) |
| 8 | Deliverables named and valued | A list of features | Named deliverables | Benefit-led names, stated values, and justification for each value |
| 9 | Pricing is value-based and premium | Priced at or below market average | Above average but not anchored | Premium, anchored, stated value is 10x price, terms defined |
| 10 | Scarcity | None, or fake | Present but weak or unexplained | Real, specific, explained (spots, cohort, capacity) |
| 11 | Urgency | None, or fake | A deadline exists | A real, enforced deadline with a reason |
| 12 | Bonuses | None, or a discount instead | Bonuses listed without purpose | Each bonus kills a named objection, is valued, and is scarce or exclusive |
| 13 | Guarantee | None | Generic refund | Named, specific "if X in Y then Z", matched to the real obstacle |
| 14 | Name | Descriptive label or product name | Uses one MAGIC element | Two or three MAGIC elements, short, specific |
| 15 | Decommoditised | Directly comparable to competitors | Some differentiation | Nothing else on the market matches the combination; the alternative is "nothing" |

## Reading the total

- 0 to 10: the offer is a commodity. Rebuild from Stage 1.
- 11 to 20: a good offer with leaks. Fix the lowest-scoring lines in this order: avatar and dream outcome first, then problems and deliverables, then guarantee, then scarcity and urgency, then name.
- 21 to 30: a strong offer. Focus on pricing up, tiering with restricted supply, and rotating the wrapper.

## Common leaks and the fix

| Symptom the user reports | Likely leak | First fix |
|---|---|---|
| "Nobody is buying" | Avatar too broad or no dream outcome | Niche down, rewrite the outcome in their words |
| "They say it is too expensive" | Value not communicated, no anchor, competing on price | Stack and value the deliverables, anchor, add bonuses instead of discounting |
| "They say they need to think about it" | No urgency, no risk reversal | Real deadline plus a named guarantee |
| "They buy then churn" | Time delay (no fast win), value falls below price | Engineer a first win in the first days, add ongoing new value, continuity bribe |
| "They ask for a discount" | Price seen as negotiable | Replace discounts with bonuses, hold price, tier with restricted supply |
| "Competitors undercut me" | Commodity offer | Solve the problems competitors ignore so the offer cannot be compared |
