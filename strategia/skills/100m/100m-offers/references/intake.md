# Intake questions

Ask only what you cannot infer. Group them into a single message, at most 6 questions. If the user says "just draft it" or is not around, state assumptions at the top of the brief and continue.

## Must know (build cannot proceed without these)

1. **What do you sell and to whom?** One sentence. If the avatar is vague ("small businesses", "people who want to get fit"), push once for a narrower one: industry, role, size, situation.
2. **What does the customer actually want at the end?** Not the deliverable, the outcome. If they answer with a feature, ask "and what does that get them?"
3. **Current price and how it is paid** (one-off, monthly, per project). Also the rough cost to deliver, so pricing and trim-and-stack are grounded.

## Very useful

4. **What objections or hesitations do you hear in sales conversations?** These feed the guarantee (reverse each one) and the bonuses (each bonus kills one objection).
5. **What real capacity limits do you have?** Spots, cohort dates, hours. This is where honest scarcity comes from.
6. **What proof do you have?** Results, case studies, years in business, numbers. Only real proof goes in the brief.

## Nice to have

- What competitors charge and what they include (for the price anchor and for decommoditising).
- Any existing name, brand, or tone constraints.
- Whether they want the brief as markdown only or also as .docx or .pdf.

## Critique mode

Ask for the full existing copy (sales page, pricing table, pitch script) if it was not pasted. Ask what "not working" means in numbers if they have them: traffic, calls booked, close rate, churn. Do not guess these.
