# Warm outreach (one-to-one, warm audience)

The cheapest, most reliable way to get the first customers of any new product or service, and a re-engagement engine for established ones. Everything is manual and personal, so leads per hour are low but dependable.

## The ten steps

1. **Get your list.** Phone contacts, every email account's address book, every social platform's followers and connections. Added up, this is often the first 1,000 leads.
2. **Pick a platform.** The one with the most contacts. You will hit them all eventually.
3. **Personalize the greeting.** Use something you actually know about them as the reason to reach out. Check their profiles if needed. You are checking in and providing value, not pitching. Example: "Saw you just had a baby! Congrats! How is the baby doing? How are you?"
4. **Reach out to 100 people every day.** Follow up up to three times, once per day for three days or until they respond (physical mail: once per week).
5. **When they respond, run A-C-A.** Acknowledge what they said in your own words. Compliment them, tied to a positive character trait. Ask a question that moves toward the topic your offer lives in. Example flow: "Two kids and a full-time accounting job... Supermom! So hardworking... Do you get time for yourself?" People love talking about themselves; this earns trust honestly.
6. **Make the offer, framed as a referral ask.** After 3 or 4 normal exchanges (or 3 to 4 minutes in person). The book's script pattern, filled in with the value equation (dream outcome, likelihood, time, effort):
   "By the way, do you know anybody who is [struggle] looking to [dream outcome] in [time frame]? I'm taking on five case studies for free, because that's all I can handle. I just want testimonials for my [service/product]. I help them [dream outcome] without [effort and sacrifice]. I even guarantee they get [dream outcome] or I work with them until they do. [One or two real proof lines if they exist.] Does anyone you like come to mind? ... (if no) Haha, well, does anyone you hate come to mind?"
   You are not asking them to buy. You are asking who they know. Many say "what about me?". Three winning outcomes: they show interest, they refer, or both.
   Short form for tight spaces: "I help [ideal customer] get [dream outcome] in [time period] without [effort and sacrifice] and [proof or guarantee]."
7. **Make it free, with terms.** "Since I'm only taking on five people, I can give you all the attention you need to get brag-worthy results. And I'll give it all for free so long as you promise to: 1) use it, 2) give me feedback, and 3) leave a killer review if you think it deserves one. Does that sound fair?" The first five free exist for reps, forgiveness while you improve, feedback, and reviews/referrals. Free customers pay three ways: they convert, they refer, their testimonials sell.
8. **Start back at the top** on the next-biggest platform. 1,000 leads at 100/day is ten working days, a month with follow-ups.
9. **Start charging once people start referring.** That is the litmus test. Ladder: 80% off for the next five, then 60% off, then 40%, raising roughly 20% every five until the sweet spot. "Prices go up every five" is honest urgency.
10. **Keep the list warm.** Regular value by email and social. Probe with Dean Jackson's 9-word email: "Are you still looking to [four-word desire]?" No images, no links, just the question. Replies are engaged leads and jump to the top of the outreach queue.

## Handling no

The most expensive part of a free offer is the hidden cost: time, effort, sacrifice. If people refuse a free thing, either they do not want the outcome, do not believe you, or the hidden costs are too high. Ask "why?", then: "What would I have to do to make it worth it for you to continue?" Mindset from the book: "Yeses give me opportunity. Nos give me feedback. Either way, I win."

## Benchmarks (book figures, label them)

- ~1 in 5 warm contacts engage (100 reach outs → ~20 replies).
- ~1 in 5 of engagers take the free offer (→ ~4).
- ~1 in 4 free-offer takers convert to paid later (→ ~1 customer per 100 reach outs).
- The book's $100K math: 500 reach outs per week, 1% of list buys a $400 offer → 5 customers/week → ~$104,000/year.
- Time floor when starting: think four hours per day minimum on getting customers.

## Limits

Two limiters: your time, and the size of the warm audience. When the list runs out, either grow it (posting content) or go cold. Both keep warm outreach running underneath.
