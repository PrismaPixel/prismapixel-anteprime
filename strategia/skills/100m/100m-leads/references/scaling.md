# Scaling: More, Better, New, the Rule of 100, Open To Goal, and the roadmap

## The saturation fallacy

Entrepreneurs reach $1M to $3M/year on one platform, one method, one audience, and declare the market "tapped" (the book's example: an agency owner "saturating" a $15.1B chiropractor industry with $30k/month on one platform). The size-of-the-pie fallacy: mistaking your tiny advertising slice for the whole market. "Saying 'I'm as big as I can get' is much easier than saying 'I'm not as good at advertising as I thought.'"

## More, Better, New, in that order

**More**: double the inputs before changing anything. The biggest increases usually come from advertising more. The diagnostic question the book asks every portfolio company: "What's stopping you from doing ten times what you're currently doing?" Sometimes nothing. Answer it and you know your next move.

**The Rule of 100**: 100 primary actions per day, every day, for 100 days.
- Warm outreach: 100 reach outs/day.
- Content: 100 minutes/day creating; at least one post/day.
- Cold outreach: 100 reach outs/day (use automation; response rates are lower).
- Paid ads: 100 minutes/day making ads, 100 days straight of running them, at the budget reversed from the customer goal, aiming for Client Financed Acquisition.

**Open To Goal** (Rule of 100 on steroids): commit to outcomes per day, not actions. The book's gym chain example: five new members per day no matter what; done by lunch, leave early; 18 hours, so be it. "Give up the idea of 'doing your best.' Instead, do what is required."

**Better**: only possible through testing, and testing is aimed at the **constraint**: the funnel step where the most leads drop off. The book's worked math: 30% opt in, 5% apply, 50% schedule; +5 points on the middle step doubles lead flow while +5 on the first step adds 16%. Rules: one test per week per platform; keep a log of every test so you never restart from zero; if four tries in a month cannot beat the control, move to the next constraint. More and better alternate: do more until it breaks, fix the break, do more again.

**New**: only when the returns from more and better fall below what a new placement would earn. Order, nine times out of ten: new placements on your current platform → your known placements on a new platform → new placements there → a new Core Four method. Get it going, measure, then apply more-better to it.

## Volume reality checks (use in critiques)

- The flyer story: 300 flyers and zero leads was 1/1500th of the required effort; the mentor tested with 5,000 and ran 5,000 per day for a month on a winner. Response rules of thumb: half a percent decent, one percent a winner.
- "I reached out to 100 people over six weeks and got one customer" is 1/42 of the prescribed volume. It was 100 per day.
- The failure promise, inverted: "I would never let effort be the reason anything didn't work for me."

## The one-page advertising plan (five steps)

1. Pick the lead type to get: customers, affiliates, employees, or agencies.
2. Pick Rule of 100 or Open To Goal, and commit to the daily actions.
3. Fill out the daily checklist for that action.
4. Do it daily until you can afford to pay someone else to do it.
5. Then repeat the process with employees as the new lead type, until you have the help you need. Scale again.

## The roadmap: seven levels (place the user honestly)

1. **Friends know what you sell.** One offer, one avatar, one platform. Primary action: warm outreach.
2. **Everyone you know hears consistently.** You know your inputs per engaged lead; max out your personal capacity. Primary actions: all the warm outreach and content you can sustain.
3. **Employees help you advertise.** You are maxed; the platform is not. Hire people to advertise profitably on your behalf.
4. **Product good enough for consistent referrals.** Benchmark: 25%+ of customers from referrals; the book updates product from feedback every two weeks at this level. "This is where most people mess up. They let their product slip and never recover."
5. **More places, more ways, more people.** New audiences on the best platform, then all placements and media types, then a second platform, lead getter, or method. Two profitable methods on multiple platforms.
6. **Hire killers.** Veteran executives who grow channels without you.
7. Reserved (the book: "I'll come back and edit this chapter once I cross a billion").

Timeline honesty from the book: five to ten years for owners who know what to do; a new lead source takes three to six months to crack even for Hormozi. Pick a strategy and stick with it for a decade. Set aside some percentage of ad budget (1 to 10%) for testing new things with no expected return.

## Which methods to combine

After warm outreach seeds the customer base: more time than money → post content; more money than time → cold outreach or paid ads. All methods compound each other; content warms every other channel. You only need one method done to the hilt: pick one, max it out, do more, do better, do new.
