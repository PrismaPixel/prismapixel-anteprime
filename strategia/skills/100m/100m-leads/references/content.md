# Post free content (one-to-many, warm audience)

Content grows the warm audience; the audience, not the content, is the compounding asset. Free content also multiplies every other channel: the book's survey found 78% of clients had consumed at least two long-form pieces before booking a call, even when paid ads got the credit.

## The content unit: hook, retain, reward

The smallest amount of material that hooks, retains, and rewards attention. A meme or one sentence can do all three at once. Longer content is content units linked together; "there is no such thing as too long, only too boring."

### Hook (measured by the % of people who start consuming)
Three levers: topics, headlines, format.
- **Topics** come best from lived experience, five sources: far past (lessons, "give them the story without the scar"), recent past (do stuff, then talk about what you did; mine your calendar and notes; testimonials and case studies live here), present (capture ideas the moment they occur), trending (go where the attention is, apply your expertise to it), manufactured (make something happen to talk about: "I lived on $100 for a month").
- **Headlines**: include at least one or two of the seven news components: recency, relevancy, celebrity, proximity, conflict, unusual, ongoing.
- **Format**: match what already works on the platform; people consume what looks like things that rewarded them before. Format for the platform first, then tweak for your avatar.

### Retain (keep them consuming)
Drive curiosity by embedding questions. Three shapes:
- **Lists**: themed items in any order ("28 Ways To Stay Poor"). Flexible, less explicit reward. Stating the count up front retains longer.
- **Steps**: ordered actions to a result ("How I Create a Headline in 7 Steps"). Less flexible, explicit reward.
- **Stories**: events real or imagined with a takeaway. Interweave all three freely.

### Reward (satisfy the reason they came)
Quality is value per second. Rewarding means matching or exceeding the expectation the hook set: deliver all seven ways when you promised seven, to the audience the hook called. The audience decides, not you; the test is whether the audience grows. If it is not growing, the content is not good enough yet.

## Monetization: the give:ask ratio

- Mature-platform minimum, from the book: TV runs ~13 minutes of ads per hour (~3.5:1 give:ask); Facebook ~4 posts per ad. Treat give-give-give-ask as the floor.
- Growing accounts should give far more: give until they ask. Never asking makes people ask you, and "give in public, ask in private" attracts the best customers without slowing growth. "The moment you start asking for money is the moment you decide to slow down your growth."
- Two ask strategies: **integrated** (short ask inside every piece, like a podcast running 90 seconds of ads in an hour, well above 3:1; place the CTA after a valuable moment or at the end, add a second placement only if growth does not slow) and **intermittent** (10 give posts, ask on the 11th). Short-form platforms favor intermittent; long-form favors integrated.
- What to advertise: the core offer or the lead magnet, nothing else. If unsure, the lead magnet; it is lower risk. Book's example ask: "I have 11 more tips that have helped me do this. Go to my site to grab a pretty visual of them." Core-offer ask follows the value-equation script from the warm outreach file.

## Scaling: depth then width, or width then depth

- **Depth first**: maximize one platform (book ceilings: short form up to 10 posts/day per platform; long form up to 5 days/week), then add the next while maintaining. Compounds fastest, fewest resources, but single-platform risk: platforms change rules and ban accounts, and one channel can kill the business if it dies.
- **Width first**: get on every relevant platform posting regularly, repurpose one piece across all similar platforms, then maximize all at once. Broader reach sooner, but more labor and a risk of thin content everywhere.
- Both are right. Start today either way.

## Seven lessons from the book

1. "How I" beats "How to", especially early. Experience cannot be argued with.
2. People need reminding more than teaching. Promote the same thing daily; a fifth of the audience never saw it (the book's own book-post survey).
3. Puddles, ponds, lakes, oceans: narrow the audience to what you do and where until you are king of the puddle, then widen.
4. Content arms your salespeople: keep a labelled "greatest hits" list mapped to the objection each piece resolves; send before and after calls.
5. Free content retains paying customers; they count it in their ROI.
6. Attention spans are not shorter; standards are higher.
7. Post manually, not pre-scheduled; the book's experience is manual posts perform better.

## Benchmarks

Measure monthly: total followers/reach, and month-over-month growth rate. Control inputs (posting cadence, ask cadence), then Do. Not. Stop. The book's patience example: twice-weekly podcast for four years before the Top 100; Top 10 US business podcast in year five. Everyone starts at zero; give time, time.

## First post

If you have been providing value to people for years, your first post can be an ask. If it produces nothing, give for a while and ask again once you have earned it.
